Complete ground truth for all 19 supplied USDA Agricultural Research Service Technology Center mechanical sheets: title/sheet/scale and full-page review; six strict M-601 schedules with 30 literal rows and rich secondary schedule inventories; 157 literal air-valve applications; M-803 through M-805 controller matrices with 98 cell-bounded rows plus a 19-row dual-render visual boiler-matrix transcription; BAS/network and sequences from M-801 through M-805; mapped template sensor/thermostat contexts; and explicit source limitations. The record does not invent a final panel/terminal count, city/state, global equipment multiplier, control-valve selection, or damper-actuator schedule.

## Reading this record

- All 19 supplied pages have a saved full-page 144 DPI Poppler visual review. Critical M-803 through M-805 matrix and sequence regions received additional visual review; literal strict cells and bounded assertions are independently checked against retained MuPDF and Poppler captures.
- M-601's six strict schedule tables retain 30 marks. Rich schedule inventory records 3 fan coils, 19 fans, 13 air-valve types, 10 attenuators, 2 unit heaters, 8 air terminals, 8 tanks, 6 air devices, 2 PRVs, and all 157 east/west laboratory air-valve application rows without equating their source rows to actuator or BAS-I/O quantities.
- The M-803 chiller/primary-secondary matrix has 23 dual-text-engine rows, and M-804/M-805 have 75 more. M-803's 19-row boiler matrix uses outline glyphs that are absent from both raw text captures, so it is retained as a matching-MuPDF/Poppler visual transcription rather than presented as a raw-cell assertion.
- Printed controller matrix circles are preserved by their AI/DI/AO/DO category and system template. They are logical/physical matrix assignments; the sheets say virtual points are incomplete, so no final controller-card, wiring, terminal, or installed-I/O total is claimed.
- The schedules and sequences have valve/damper control context, but no control-valve schedule or damper-actuator schedule publishes Cv, actuator selection/torque, signal, fail action, or a global installed quantity.

- sequence of operations:
  - heating water boiler plant M803:
    - The hot-water plant is two boilers (B-1A/B-1B) and two VFD hot-water pumps (P-3A/P-3B) serving AHUs/roof-top-unit preheat coils and VAV reheat coils.
    - BAS/manual override can start and stop the hot-water plant; an adjustable one-minute delay plus differential-pressure proof precedes sequencing-controller enablement.
    - Pumps rotate weekly by least runtime; the lead isolation valve opens, lag isolation valve closes, one pump operates, and an alarming lead-pump failure starts the lag pump.
    - BAS uses current switches for proof and alarms if actual status departs BAS start/stop after the adjustable proving period.
    - VFD speed is controlled using DPS-1 sensed differential pressure against the DPS-2/DPS-3 desired setpoint; the TAB contractor supplies minimum-output-vector-graph settings for defined operating DP settings.
    - Boilers rotate weekly by least runtime. In remote, their sequencing controllers control boiler temperature/reset/firing/sequence; local leaves the boiler to internal control.
    - The lead boiler starts low-fire on low HWS temperature; 60% firing plus still-low HWS after adjustable 20 minutes enables the next boiler, while all enabled boilers modulate in parallel.
    - HWS reset uses TS-OA. Differential-pressure reset is 25 psi to 5 psi adjustable; coil-valve position determines the critical zone, with stated 85%, 95%, and 97% damper-position reset logic.
    - Gas pressure is displayed at BAS. A hardwired manual-mushroom emergency interface shuts down boilers independent of BMS programming and creates a BMS alarm. BAS sends variable HWS setpoint to each sequencing controller.
  - chilled water and ice storage M803:
    - The chilled-water system is manually started and runs continuously.
    - The lead primary pump runs continuously with the lead chiller; failure starts the second pump/alarm and supports manual or timed lead rotation.
    - A differential-pressure sensor at each chiller measures evaporator supply-to-return drop; active-chiller pressure controls primary-pump speed. When both chillers run, both signals are averaged and both primary pumps run in tandem.
    - Chiller isolation opens before differential-pressure-switch flow proof enables internal controls; manufacturer controls operate compressors/condenser fans while DDC can adjust power-limit setting.
    - Secondary pumps start continuously, use a 4-20 mA DC VFD speed command, operate in unison to maintain differential pressure, stage on low DP, and start the next pump on failure/alarm. Communication failure fixes pump speed.
    - V-2 keeps secondary CHW no colder than 5.5 C; it closes to the secondary circuit when both secondary pumps are off, bypassing primary water back to the chiller.
    - The load monitor uses secondary CHW flow plus building supply/return temperature.
    - Ice modes are make ice, use ice, and bypass. Make ice needs low-demand billing and inventory no greater than 80%; use ice needs high-demand billing, inventory greater than 25%, and at least one secondary pump. All modes may be commanded from the operator workstation.
    - In make-ice mode, the lead primary pump/chiller run and V-1 opens to storage; storage discharge reaches -2.2 C before change to use-ice or bypass. In use-ice mode V-1 maintains 5.5 C primary supply. In bypass V-1 closes to storage; both primary pumps/chillers stop if both secondary pumps stop.
